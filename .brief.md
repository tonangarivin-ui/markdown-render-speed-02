The spec's link rules, in the order that decides the answer:
- Inline link: `[text](destination "title")`. The destination is a `<...>` span, or a run of non-space
  balanced-parenthesis characters. NOTHING inside is percent-encoded or entity-decoded: copy it as written.
  A `\` escapes the next punctuation character, and the escaped character is written without the backslash.
- The title may be in `"..."`, `'...'` or `(...)` and its own escapes are processed the same way.
- A link is only a link if the WHOLE `[...](...)` and its brackets are balanced and the text part contains no
  unescaped `[` at the outer level. `[a[b](c)` is not a link; `[a\[b](c)` is.
- Reference links: `[text][label]`, `[text][]` (label is the text), `[label]` (shortcut). The label is matched
  case-insensitively after collapsing internal whitespace runs to one space and trimming. A full or collapsed
  reference with NO matching definition is NOT a link at all - the brackets stay literal text.
- A shortcut `[label]` is a link only when the label matches a definition; otherwise the brackets stay literal.
- A link reference definition is `[label]: destination "title"` at the start of a line with at most 3 spaces of
  indentation. The destination may be on the NEXT line. The title may also be on the next line. A definition
  consumes its own lines and produces NO output; it must be followed by a blank line or the end of the block.
- A definition inside a paragraph's first lines is stripped from the paragraph before rendering.
- Images use the same destination/title rules; alt text is the plain-text rendering of the inner content.

Known facts from measurement - do not undo them:
- named character references are NEVER decoded (`&copy;` stays `&copy;`); the two NUMERIC forms decode
  (`&#42;` -> `*`), and an invalid code point becomes U+FFFD; any other `&` becomes `&amp;`.
- link destinations, image sources and autolink URLs are copied from the source as-is. Do NOT percent-encode.
